For KDG as a metric:
A single aggregate KDG number masks domain heterogeneity. A model could have KDG=0.05 overall but KDG=0.40 in the health 
domain and KDG=0.00 in history/geography. Your current paper reports domain-level S1 rates (§3.3) but not domain-level KDG. 
The Phase 4 data gives you the opportunity to compute domain-specific KDG for the first time — and the Llama 70B result would
show a striking pattern: near-zero KDG_S1 everywhere, but elevated KDG_R specifically in health.

For the alignment interpretation:
The 70B model appears to have learned a more nuanced alignment strategy than 8B — it corrects false premises in most domains 
(calibration-based) but still refuses in health/medical (constraint-based). This is arguably the right behavior: "vaccines 
cause autism" is genuinely dangerous misinformation, not just a factual error. The 8B model over-applied the constraint 
mechanism broadly; the 70B model applies it selectively.

For the paper:
This is a new finding worth adding to the scale section. The headline isn't just "KDG decreases with scale" — it's "alignment
strategy becomes more domain-appropriate with scale: constraint-based behavior narrows to genuinely high-risk domains while 
calibration-based behavior expands to cover the rest." That's a more nuanced and interesting claim than a simple scaling law.
